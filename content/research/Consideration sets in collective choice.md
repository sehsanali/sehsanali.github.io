**Abstract:** We explore how agents with limited attention may deliberate to consider items collectively. We We consider agents with limited attention who deliberate and form a collective consideration set. We envisage purely communicative deliberation processes where agents reveal the contents of their individual consideration sets, drawn randomly and independently from a menu via an attention rule. Our main result shows that if the deliberation process satisfies a monotonicity property, then the collective discovers the entire menu with certainty as a larger number of agents come together. We provide a groupthink heuristic which does not satisfy the monotonicity property and show that crowds may not grow wiser.
